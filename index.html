One inspection, start to finish

Real timings from a quarterly inspection on a two-bedroom flat.

  1. Mon 08:14

    You schedule it

    Pick a date, or set it to repeat every quarter and stop thinking about it. Takes about two minutes.

  2. Tue 09:30

    A verified inspector arrives

    They work the checklist room by room on their phone, marking each item and photographing as they go. Their draft survives a dead signal.

  3. Tue 17:00

    The report reaches you

    Every room, every item, every photo, and anything flagged — with the inspector's own words. Under 48 hours from submission, measured.

  4. Wed 11:02

    You decide

    Approve the repair or decline it with a reason. Both are recorded. Until you choose, nothing moves and nobody is sent.

  5. Wed 14:40

    A verified provider takes it

    You watch the job move — requested, assigned, in progress, completed — and it closes with after-photos, not a phone call.

Four people touch your property. Each one is fenced in.

These limits are enforced where the data lives, not hidden in the interface. Nobody sees a screen they shouldn't, because nobody can query it.

What each role can see and cannot do
RoleSeesCannot
You, the owner Every property you own, every report, every flagged issue, and the full history of who did what. Be bypassed. A repair without your approval does not exist.
Inspector Only the inspections assigned to them, and only for the visit window. See your other properties, or approve anything they flag.
Admin Platform-wide activity, so assignments and verification never stall. Approve a repair for you, or assign an unverified provider.
Service provider Only the job they accepted, with the issue photos attached to it. Be assigned at all until an admin has verified them.

Why not just ask your cousin to check?

Because you already tried, and here is where each of these runs out.

A relative drops by
Kind of them. No checklist, no photos, nothing written down, and easy to forget for three months.
Property management software
Built to collect rent and log tenant tickets. It has no opinion on whether your bathroom is leaking.
A video call with a local agent
You see exactly where the camera points, nothing is timestamped, and you are trusting a narration.
Hiring a one-off inspector
One report, once. No schedule, no approval trail, and no one to actually carry out the repair afterwards.

Trust here is a constraint, not a promise

Three things the platform will not let anyone do, including us.

An unverified provider cannot be assigned

The assignment is refused outright unless that provider's verification flag is true. Not greyed out in a dropdown — refused, with an error the admin has to read.

No decision can be quietly edited

Every approval, assignment and status change writes one permanent line. Those lines can be read and added to. They cannot be changed or deleted by anyone.

48 hours is measured, not estimated

The clock starts when the inspector submits the checklist and stops when your report is ready. Both timestamps are on the report.
